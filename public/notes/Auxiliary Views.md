# Auxiliary Views

#mmet380 #ch2 #concept

## In Plain English

Sometimes a part has a slanted surface — a boss at an angle, an angled cut, a chamfered flange. In a standard front, top, or side view, that surface appears *foreshortened*: it looks smaller and distorted than it really is. You cannot dimension or fully inspect a foreshortened surface. An auxiliary view solves this by adding a **fourth projection plane** that is parallel to the slanted surface, so the view you get is the undistorted, true-shape view of just that face.

## Why They Are Needed

> An auxiliary view is drawn at a special projected angle to show the **true shape** of an oblique (slanted) part.

- Standard views (top, front, side) are only undistorted for surfaces **perpendicular to the line of sight**
- Any surface that is oblique to all three principal planes appears foreshortened in every standard view
- Only a projection **perpendicular to the oblique surface** reveals its true dimensions

## How to Draw an Auxiliary View

1. **Identify** the oblique surface in the front view (or another standard view)
2. **Determine the viewing direction** — it must be **perpendicular** to the oblique surface
3. **Project** lines from the oblique surface in that direction onto the auxiliary plane
4. The auxiliary view is "folded" 90° from the view it is derived from (e.g., 90° from the front view)
5. **Show only the oblique surface** in the auxiliary view — you may omit the rest of the part, though showing it is acceptable

> [!tip] Partial Auxiliary Views
> It is standard practice (and often cleaner) to show **only the oblique surface** in the auxiliary view, not the complete outline of the part. This avoids confusing foreshortened outlines.

## Key Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Viewing direction | Must be perpendicular to the oblique surface |
| Fold angle | Auxiliary view is 90° from the parent view |
| Content | Typically shows **only the oblique surface** |
| Dimensions | True dimensions can only be taken from the auxiliary view, not from standard views |
| Alignment | Projection lines remain parallel between parent view and auxiliary view |

## Example Scenario

A part has a slanted boss at 30° from vertical:

- Front view: the boss face appears as a foreshortened trapezoid — you cannot measure its true width or height
- Auxiliary view (projected perpendicular to the boss face): the boss appears as a **true rectangle** — you can dimension it correctly

## Common Pitfalls

> [!warning] Pitfalls
> - **Measuring from the wrong view**: Never take dimensions of an oblique surface from a standard view — use the auxiliary view.
> - **Wrong projection angle**: The auxiliary view must align with the slant, not just be placed at any convenient angle.
> - **Omitting the alignment lines**: Projection lines between the parent view and the auxiliary view must remain parallel.
