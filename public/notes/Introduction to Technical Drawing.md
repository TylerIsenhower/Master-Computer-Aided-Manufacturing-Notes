# Introduction to Technical Drawing

#mmet380 #ch2 #concept

## In Plain English

Before anything is built, it has to be drawn. Technical drawing is the universal language engineers use to transfer an idea in someone's head into a set of instructions a machinist or manufacturer can act on without ambiguity. A sketch on a napkin is great for brainstorming; a proper technical drawing is what survives the hand-off to production.

## Purpose of Technical Drawing

> Graphics is a universal and powerful tool to communicate and document ideas. Technical drawing allows us to follow ideas from concept to final products.

Technical drawing fits into the broader **Product Design Process**:

1. **Concept** — ideation and rough sketches
2. **Preliminary design** — first technical drawings with basic geometry
3. **Detailed design** — fully dimensioned, toleranced engineering drawings
4. **Manufacturing** — drawings drive machining, fabrication, and assembly
5. **Documentation** — drawings serve as the legal and contractual record of the design

## Why Drawings Must Be Unambiguous

A single sketch from one angle cannot fully define a 3D object — many different shapes can produce the same silhouette. Technical drawing solves this by:

- Using **standardized projection conventions** so reader and drafter share the same rules
- Including **multiple views** to eliminate geometric ambiguity
- Adding **dimensions and tolerances** so every measurement is explicit
- Specifying **line types** to distinguish visible edges, hidden features, center lines, and reference guides

## Line Types

| Line Type | Appearance | Purpose |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Visible line** | Solid, thick | Edges and contours you can see from the viewing direction |
| **Hidden line** | Dashed | Edges obscured by solid material |
| **Center line** | Long–short–long dash, thin | Axes of symmetry, hole centers; extends **beyond** part contour |
| **Guide line** | Very light, thin | Construction reference; **omitted** from final drawing |

> [!tip] Hidden vs. Center Lines
> Hidden lines **stop** at part contours. Center lines **extend** slightly beyond them. Getting this wrong is one of the most common drawing errors.

## The Coordinate System

All main lines in a 2D technical drawing are parallel to the **x** or **y** axis of the drawing plane. A circle appears:
- As a **true circle** when viewed along the axis the circle lies in
- As a **line** (collapsed) when viewed perpendicular to its axis
