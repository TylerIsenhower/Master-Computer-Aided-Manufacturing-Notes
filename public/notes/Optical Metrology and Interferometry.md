# Optical Metrology and Interferometry

#mmet380 #ch8 #concept

## In Plain English

Light waves are surprisingly good rulers. When two light waves overlap, they create a "fringe pattern" (it looks like a contour map). An **Optical Flat** is a piece of glass that is perfectly flat. When you place it on a part and shine a light, the pattern of lines tells you exactly how flat the part is. If the lines are straight, the part is flat. If they are curved, the part is wavy. **Interferometry** is a computerized version of this that can measure roughness at the scale of single nanometers.

## 1. Optical Flats
- **Principle:** Uses the combination of incoming and reflected light beams of a specific wavelength to create a "fringe pattern."
- **Visual Interpretation:**
    - **Straight, parallel lines:** Part is perfectly flat.
    - **Curved lines:** Part has a spherical or wavy error.
- **Application:** Checking the flatness of precision gages and seals.

## 2. Interferometry
- **Principle:** An interferometer captures the surface contour by analyzing the interference patterns digitally.
- **Capabilities:** Measures surface roughness, flatness, and angles between features.
- **Benefit:** Extremely high resolution; can measure features that are too small for a physical probe.

## Summary Table

| Tool | Measurement | Resolution |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **Optical Flat** | Flatness | High (visual) |
| **Interferometer** | 3D Contour / Roughness | **Extreme** (sub-micron) |
