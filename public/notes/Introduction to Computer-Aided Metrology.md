# Introduction to Computer-Aided Metrology

#mmet380 #ch8 #concept

## In Plain English

In Chapter 7, we learned about manual tools like calipers and dial indicators. These are great for a single part, but if you are making 10,000 parts a day, a person can't measure them all by hand. **Computer-Aided Metrology** is the solution. It uses sensors, cameras, and computers to measure parts automatically. It is much faster, more accurate, and can handle the complex data needed to verify that a part actually follows all the "circles and boxes" of the GD&T symbols we learned earlier.

## The Role of Metrology in CAM

Computer-Aided Metrology is a critical pillar of **Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM)**.
- **Data Collection:** Automatically recording measurements for quality control records.
- **Reversed Engineering:** Scanning an existing physical part to create a digital CAD model.
- **GD&T Verification:** Providing the "high-density" data required to check features like surface profile or total runout.

## Why Automate?

1. **Mass Production:** Humans are too slow for high-volume assembly lines.
2. **Capability:** Some features (like internal voids or 3D contours) are physically impossible to measure with manual hand tools.
3. **Consistency:** Computers don't get tired or make "human errors" in reading a scale.
