# Position and Speed Control

#mmet380 #ch3 #concept

## In Plain English

A CNC controller doesn't think in inches or millimeters; it thinks in electrical pulses. Every time the controller sends one pulse to a motor, the motor turns a tiny fraction of a degree, moving the machine table a microscopic distance. That minimum possible movement is the machine's "resolution," known as the BLU. If you want the table to move 1 inch, the computer calculates how many pulses that requires and fires them off. If you want the table to move fast, it fires the pulses rapidly (high frequency). Position is controlled by the *number* of pulses; speed is controlled by the *frequency* of the pulses.

## Linear Interpolation (Straight Line Motion)

A leadscrew converts the rotation of a motor into linear motion. The smallest distance the table can move forward with a single electrical pulse is called the **Basic Length Unit (BLU)**.

### Position Control
To move the table a certain distance, the controller calculates the required number of pulses:
$$ \text{Number of pulses} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{BLU}} $$

### Speed Control
By taking the derivative with respect to time, position becomes speed, and the number of pulses becomes the pulse frequency (pulses per unit time):
$$ \text{Pulse Frequency} = \frac{\text{Speed}}{\text{BLU}} $$

> [!warning] Unit Consistency
> Always ensure your time units match! CNC speeds are typically given in inches per minute (in/min), but pulse frequency might be asked for in Hertz (pulses per second) or pulses per minute. Convert carefully.

## Angular Interpolation (Rotational Motion)

For a rotary table or spindle, the motor has a **Basic Angular Unit (BAU)**, defined as the minimum rotation angle generated by one pulse.

### Angular Position Control
$$ \text{Number of pulses} = \frac{\text{Angle}}{\text{BAU}} $$

### Angular Speed Control
$$ \text{Pulse Frequency} = \frac{\text{Angular Speed}}{\text{BAU}} $$

## Worked Examples

> [!example]- Examples (click to expand)
> **Linear Position Example:**
> - **Given:** BLU = 0.001 in/pulse. Move distance = 0.5 inches.
> - **Find:** Required number of pulses.
> - **Solution:** $\text{Pulses} = 0.5 \text{ in} / 0.001 \text{ in/pulse} = 500 \text{ pulses}$.
> 
> **Linear Speed Example:**
> - **Given:** BLU = 0.001 in/pulse. Target speed = 20 in/min.
> - **Find:** Required pulse frequency in pulses/min.
> - **Solution:** $\text{Freq} = 20 \text{ in/min} / 0.001 \text{ in/pulse} = 20,000 \text{ pulses/min}$. (Note: the raw text derived 333 pulses/sec, which is $20,000 / 60$).
>
> **Angular Speed Example:**
> - **Given:** BAU = 0.1°/pulse. Target rotation speed = 30°/min.
> - **Find:** Required pulse frequency in pulses/sec (Hz).
> - **Solution:** First, convert speed to degrees/sec: $30 / 60 = 0.5 \text{ deg/sec}$. Then, $\text{Freq} = 0.5 / 0.1 = 5 \text{ Hz (pulses/sec)}$.
