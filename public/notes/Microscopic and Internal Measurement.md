# Microscopic and Internal Measurement

#mmet380 #ch8 #concept

## In Plain English

Standard cameras and probes can only see the outside of a part. If you need to see the individual fibers in a composite material, or find a tiny air bubble (void) trapped inside a heavy engine casting, you need specialized tech. **SEM** (Scanning Electron Microscopy) uses a beam of electrons to create a 3D-looking image at a magnification much higher than any microscope. **CT Scanning** (like in a hospital) uses X-rays to look *through* the part, letting you find internal cracks or holes without cutting the part open.

## 1. Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)
- **Principle:** Fires a beam of electrons at the surface and collects the "scattered" electrons to build an image.
- **Capabilities:** Extremely high magnification (can see individual grains/fibers). 
- **Limitation:** Mostly provides a 2D image; estimating 3D dimensions can have significant error.

## 2. Computed Tomography (CT)
- **Principle:** Uses X-rays to take "slices" of an object. These slices are then combined into a 3D digital model.
- **Capabilities:** Can measure internal features and detect defects (voids, cracks, inclusions) in heavy materials like cast iron.
- **Benefit:** **Non-destructive testing** (you don't have to break the part to see inside).

## Summary Table

| Technology | Probe | Best For... |
|------------|-------|-------------|
| **SEM** | Electrons | Microscopic surface details, fiber composites. |
| **CT** | X-Rays | Internal features, voids in castings, assembly checks. |
