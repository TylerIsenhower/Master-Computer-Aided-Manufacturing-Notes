# Hole Making and Specialized Operations

#mmet380 #ch6 #concept

## In Plain English

Drilling a hole isn't always a one-step process. If you need a hole that is perfectly straight, smooth, and precisely sized, you have to follow a sequence: first define the center, then drill the hole, then enlarge it, then straighten it, and finally polish it. This note covers those steps, along with "deburring"—the process of removing the sharp, ugly "burrs" (leftover metal flakes) that appear on the edges of parts after machining.

## The Hole Making Sequence

To create a high-quality hole, follow these steps:

1. **Center Drill:** Creates a small indent to ensure the main drill doesn't "walk" or drift.
2. **Drill:** Forms the initial hole.
3. **Bore:** Enlarges the hole and ensures it is concentric.
4. **Ream:** Straightens the hole and brings it to final precision size.
5. **Hone:** Uses internal grinding to create a mirror-smooth surface finish.

### Other Hole Features
- **Tapping:** Cutting internal threads.
- **Counter-boring:** Enlarging the top of a hole to hide a bolt head.
- **Counter-sinking:** Creating a cone-shaped top for flat-head screws.

## Deburring

**Burrs** are undesirable flakes of plastically deformed material left on the part. They must be removed for safety and function.

| Technique | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Manual** | Operator uses a file or scraper. |
| **Mechanical** | Wire brushing, abrasive tumbling, or abrasive flow. |
| **Thermal** | A short blast of high energy melts the thin burr but leaves the part intact. |
| **Electrochemical**| Using the ECM process to dissolve the burr. |

## Buffing

Buffing uses a rotating wheel and an abrasive compound to "level" the microscopic peaks on a surface. This is used for parts requiring a high-gloss finish, such as knee implants or aesthetic hardware.
